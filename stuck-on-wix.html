Sound familiar?Four ways they keep you

How they keep you.

  1. 01

    No export button

    Website builders don't let you take your site with you. The design, the pages, the setup — none of it can be downloaded or moved. Leaving means starting over, and they know most people won't.
  2. 02

    The renewal ambush

    Year one is cheap. Then the renewal lands — often 50% higher, sometimes double — with a couple weeks' notice. By then your whole business runs on their platform, so you pay.
  3. 03

    The domain hostage

    Bought your domain through the builder? Some of them, Wix included, never let you point it anywhere else — the only way out is a registrar transfer they make as slow as the rules allow.
  4. 04

    Support that reads scripts

    When something breaks — billing, a hacked account, a store down during your busiest week — you're one ticket in a queue at a company with two hundred million users.

FAQBefore you move

Fair questions.

Q.1Will my new site look the same as my old one?

If you like your current look, we rebuild it faithfully — most clients can't spot the difference except that it loads faster. If you never loved it, this is the perfect moment for a fresh design at no extra project.

Q.2Will my website go down during the move?

No. Your old site keeps running while we build the new one. The switch itself is a DNS change that happens quietly — visitors just start landing on the better site.

Q.3Do I lose my domain or my Google ranking?

Neither. Your domain is legally yours — the platform just makes moving it annoying, and we handle that. Your rankings carry over because every old page address redirects to its new home.

Q.4What does a rescue cost?

It depends on the size of the site, but honestly — often less than another year of builder subscription fees, and afterwards your running costs drop to nearly nothing. We'll look at your site for free and give you a straight quote. Get your quote.

Q.5What about my email on the domain?

We check for email on your domain before anything moves, and carry the settings over so not a single message bounces. It's the first thing on our checklist, not an afterthought.
